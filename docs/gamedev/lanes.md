# Host-local resource lanes

`borg lane --json job submit --spec -` accepts an engine-neutral JSON `JobSpec` and returns a job ID immediately. `borg lane --json job wait ID` blocks on the supervisor's kernel lock, returns the terminal status and exits nonzero on failure. Use Borg's existing command watcher for long waits rather than repeatedly asking an agent to poll `status`. `job status [ID]`, `logs ID` (log path), `cancel ID`, `recover [--dry-run | --wait SECONDS]` (also `borg lane recover`), `resource list|status|set-capacity`, and `service ...` are also available. `--state-dir` or `BORG_LANE_DIR` overrides the host-local state root; the service root is `ROOT/services`. Do not move a live root between hosts.

The journal is atomically renamed under a stable `flock` inode and records monotone ticket sequences, pending-only full-spec coalescing, shared slot capacities, FIFO exclusion, generation-fenced leases, supervisor evidence, reasoned RAM/disk admission and quarantine. Disjoint resources admit concurrently; an earlier conflicting exclusive ticket blocks later shared work. Job and service RAM/disk reservations are rechecked under the same journal lock using `workspace::assess_budget`, with disk reservations grouped by filesystem device; rejected service attempts leave a bounded, reasoned terminal record without occupying FIFO; failed system inspection queues the request rather than granting it. Kernel completion locks identify owners after crashes; recovery kills only a verified owned cgroup or PID/start-time and quarantines anything unverifiable. Never remove stable lock files while active.

Systemd **user scopes** are the production workload and service isolation path. If no user manager is available, the runner refuses unscoped execution unless `BORG_LANE_DEGRADED=1` is explicitly set for testing. Workloads are argv arrays, not shell snippets. In the service handoff, each backend supervisor claims a `service:<id>` shared lease *before* starting and retains its FD until backend stop. Every exclusive job automatically snapshots all matching active service IDs, reserves its FIFO place in `Preparing`, requests each service to restore client settings, stop its owned backend and yield its shared lease, then rechecks release under the journal lock before granting. New backend starts and timed resumes are refused while `Preparing` or exclusive `Granted` blocks the resource. Optional pre-hooks cannot substitute for this check. For a bound service, a post hook completes before the job releases its resource; a post failure quarantines the resource and does not falsify the workload exit status. After terminal completion a detached resume worker tracks `resume_pending` until the service is actually Healthy, not just until its Resume RPC removes the yield token. The worker waits on the service's status events, outside the journal lock, for up to the service's own resume budget: its `readiness_timeout_ms` plus one health `interval_ms` and `timeout_ms`, plus 2 s to launch (the default readiness window is 120 s). A slow start that becomes Healthy inside that budget, such as an editor that needs a minute, clears `resume_pending` with no operator step. A missed readiness window (`Degraded: backend failed readiness`), `Failed`, a stopped supervisor or the expired budget leaves a reasoned `resume_error`. A transient admission wait does not end the wait early. `job recover` reports each resume it starts and retries by inspecting status without sending Resume twice. `recover --wait SECONDS` then blocks until every resume it started has cleared or finished a failed attempt; it prints each outcome (`resumed`, `failed`, `pending`) and exits nonzero unless all resumed. Inspect and resolve quarantines manually; never infer safe recovery from a PID alone.

The restricted runtime `lane_job` MCP tool supports registered nonexclusive
templates, bounded wait/status/list/cancel and submit-time watchers. It rejects
model-facing exclusives. `lane_service` supports status/lease/release/audited
reads, not raw mutation or restart. See [interfaces](interfaces.md).

Project/Worktree keys require existing absolute canonical paths; aliases are
rejected at store ingress. Per-agent dispatch caps/fair shares are not enforced
by the workspace budget helper. Host RAM admission also spans participating
lane journals through `host_admission.rs`; old binaries and unwrapped processes
do not participate. Disk reservations are grouped by filesystem device within
the lane journal, not a hard quota.

## Foreign-client handshake

An exclusive job now checks unexpired service client leases under the same journal lock that admits or renews them. It enters durable `Preparing` before preemption; clients owned by *both* the job holder's participant ID and session ID do not block it. Other clients keep the service live and produce a `foreign client lease:` reason on job wait and service status. New grants/renewals by foreign clients are rejected once a conflicting exclusive is Preparing or Granted. An explicit release wakes admission; expired leases are ignored. The default wait is `foreign_client_grace_ms` (300,000 ms); the JSON `JobSpec.foreign_client_grace_by_resource` list overrides it per requested exclusive resource, for example `[{"resource":{"scope":"Host","name":"editor"},"grace_ms":0}]`. Unspecified keys use the default, and a service bound to multiple exclusive keys uses the longest applicable grace; zero means indefinite. `borg lane job submit --spec - --foreign-lease-grace-seconds N` overrides the job-wide fallback in seconds without removing per-resource overrides. Duplicate/unrequested/nonexclusive override keys are rejected. The timer starts at the first durable Preparing transition and survives supervisor restart. Grace expiry permits preemption despite live foreign clients and is **not** a claim that clients were safely drained. Set a longer or infinite grace for interactive resources. Hooks and actual yield occur outside the journal lock; idle active hooks run after the locked client grant and roll back a failed activation under that lock.

See [regression gates](benchmark.md) for maintained public-CLI coverage.
Model-facing exclusives remain disabled independently of CLI handshake support.

## Job lifecycle controls

New `JobSpec` fields are serde-defaulted; an old spec or journal behaves as before.

- **Detached supervisor.** `lane __supervise` runs in its own session and, with a user manager, its own `borg-lane-sup-<id>` scope, so Ctrl-C or a closed agent session cannot kill a job. The workload gets `BORG_LANE_JOB` and `BORG_LANES_ROOT`.
- **Cancel and leftovers.** `job cancel` of a Preparing or running job records a request (`{"state":"cancel_requested"}`); the supervisor kills the workload, runs the post hook and ends the job `Cancelled{reason}`, and `job wait` exits 125. After any workload exit, every process left in its scope (degraded: its process group) is SIGKILLed and the group verified empty before the lease is released; the PIDs go to the evidence and `recovery.jsonl` (`killed_pids`), and a group that will not empty quarantines the resource.
- **`abandon_after_ms`.** `job wait` holds `jobs/<id>/waiters.lock` shared; with no waiter for that long since submit, a queued job is cancelled and a running one killed, with reason `abandoned: no requester`.
- **`job wait ID [--until started] [--timeout SECONDS]`.** `started` returns once the workload PID is recorded; a timeout exits 124 (`{"job_id","timed_out":true}` with `--json`) and leaves the job alone. `--json` output has `state`, `exit_code`, `reason`, `evidence` and `wait_reason`; `job status ID` adds `exit_code` and `reason` to the record.
- **Ids and units.** Ticket/job ids are UUIDv7, ordered by submission. `unit_prefix` (`[a-z][a-z0-9-]{0,23}`) names the workload scope `<prefix>-<id>.scope` (default `borg-lane`); recovery verifies that unit. `memory_swap_max_bytes` adds `MemorySwapMax=` to the scope.
- **`finish_hook`.** Runs once, detached (`lane __finish_hook`, own session and a `RuntimeMaxSec`-bounded scope) after any ending: finish, queued or running cancel, queue timeout, abandon, or recovery of a lost supervisor. `finish_hook_started_ms` is journalled before spawning; `recover` starts a hook whose ender died first; `finish_hook_outcome` records how it ended and its output goes to `jobs/<id>/finish-hook.log`. Env: `BORG_LANE_PHASE=finish`, `BORG_LANE_STATE=finished|cancelled`, `BORG_LANE_EXIT_CODE` (finished), `BORG_LANE_REASON` (cancel reason, or the evidence), plus `BORG_LANE_JOB`, `_RESOURCE`, `_LOG`, `BORG_LANES_ROOT`. Post hooks get the same state, exit code and reason. Recovery of a lost supervisor records the PIDs it kills.
- **FIFO relief.** An earlier queued ticket no longer blocks a later one when a key the later one does not request is busy, full or quarantined for it (a build waiting for its own tree does not hold another tree off a free slot). An earlier ticket that could run keeps its turn.
- **Retention.** When a ticket is created, finished and cancelled records older than `BORG_LANE_RETAIN_SECONDS` (default 86400) or beyond the newest 2000 are dropped with their job directory and lock files; quarantined records, pending service resumes and unfinished finish hooks are kept.
- **Running jobs and the journal.** Progress and CPU are written at most once a second and only when they change; otherwise a running supervisor neither locks nor reads the journal and learns of a cancel from `jobs/<id>/cancel`. Stall detection counts the whole workload: the scope's `cpu.stat` (degraded: the process group, with reaped children) and log growth.
- **Service claims of a killed supervisor.** A service lease or restart barrier whose supervisor died is released once the owner is provably gone (its unit cgroup gone or empty; otherwise its pid exited or reused), by `recover`, the next service claim or a waiting build; until then it is quarantined, and the quarantine is lifted when the proof holds. See [services.md](services.md).
